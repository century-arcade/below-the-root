Below the Root

Windham Classics, 1984. Commodore 64.

Based on the Green-sky books by Zilpha Keatley Snyder, who wrote the story. You are one of five characters and you have fifty days to find a boy trapped below the root of the great trees. 438 rooms. There is nothing to fight.

This is the original C64 game, ported to the browser. The rooms, creatures, words, pictures and music come from the original. The rules follow the original, with buttonless gliding and tune skipping added. Added: a world map, mouse and touch, gamepad, autosave, fullscreen, an optional CRT effect.

Free. Runs in your browser. Source on GitHub.

The game

Windham Classics, Spinnaker’s children’s-literature label, released Below the Root in 1984, first for the Commodore 64, then for Apple II and IBM PC. Zilpha Keatley Snyder wrote the story as a continuation of her Green-sky trilogy: Below the Root, And All Between and Until the Celebration (1975–77). Dale DeSharone, credited as Disharoon, designed and programmed the game. William Groetzinger made the art; Jim Graham, Leonard Lebow and Vince Mills were also programmers.

Neric, Genaa and Pomma are Kindar; Herd and Charn are Erdlings. Neric and Herd are strong with moderate spirit, Genaa is strong without spirit skill, Pomma has less stamina and more spirit, and Charn falls between them in stamina with moderate spirit. Their standing with the two peoples determines who will talk to them. The quest is to reach Raamo below the root within fifty days. Its exploration and growing abilities are often described as an early metroidvania. It is a pacifist game.

This port

The disks were decoded from G64 images. The 6502 program was disassembled and documented, and the art, text and music extracted. A written specification and JSON tables describe the game; the JavaScript port reads those tables, never the disassembly. Golden-image tests compare rendered screens with the original. Both attract scripts replay against VICE, with the quest script checked through its end. Walkthrough verification is still in progress.

  • World map: original room tiles, with new areas revealed as you visit.
  • Mouse and touch: steer, tap a door or double-tap a destination.
  • Gamepad: directional pad or left stick, with face buttons to select and jump.
  • Autosave: resume the quest on your next visit.
  • Fullscreen: the game fills the display.
  • CRT effect: optional scanlines, phosphor stripes and colour bleed.
  • Modern display: live status rows and tune skipping; classic display is also available.
  • Gliding: pushing sideways is enough; the button is optional.
