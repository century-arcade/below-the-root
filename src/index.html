Below the Root

Windham Classics, 1984. Commodore 64.

In Green-sky, people make their homes high in the trees. Glide between branches to visit them, and learn to sense the feelings behind a stranger’s welcome. A conversation can lead you further into the forest; a new power can help you grow a branch across a gap you couldn’t cross before. Exploring means getting to know this strange world and the people who live here.

Below the Root is a game Saul loved as a child. We’re restoring the 1984 Commodore 64 game for browsers so it can be played again and shared with others, carrying its world, art, words and music forward.

Whether you’re returning after decades or discovering Green-sky for the first time, you’re welcome here. Play free in your browser, or watch the demo for a first look around.

The game

The game’s world comes from Zilpha Keatley Snyder’s trilogy of novels: Below the Root, And All Between and Until the Celebration. Snyder helped design the game as a continuation of the books, shaping its world and writing dialogue with designer and programmer Dale DeSharone, credited as Disharoon. William Groetzinger created the art, and Windham Classics published the game in 1984.

You can begin without having read the books. A troubling dream sets a quest in motion, and the people you meet hold clues to what threatens Green-sky. Learning whom to trust matters: someone may offer food or a place to rest, while another may put you in danger. Following those clues and developing your powers gradually gives you new ways through the forest.

Restoring a way back

We’ve studied how the original game works and rebuilt it for the browser, using its own pictures, text and music. Our care goes into preserving that work and making it easier to reach today. You can start here without setting up an emulator or swapping disks.

If the controls are new or half-remembered, the Help button explains them. Use the arrow keys or WASD, or play with a mouse, touch or gamepad. The built-in map helps you find your way, revealing more outdoor areas as you explore. Visible status keeps food, rest and the passing days in view, while the forest still asks you to watch your step and plan your journey.

When it’s time for a break, autosave keeps your progress in this browser on this device so you can continue later.

For how the restoration is built and checked, see the project README and source and the specification. Problems can be reported in the issue tracker.

Others have kept Green-sky close through memories, writing and guides. Visit Resources for the creators’ accounts, other players’ recollections, maps and original materials, or help with your quest.
