<h1 id="about-title">Foreword</h1>

Once upon a time, personal computers were small.  They didn't have a hard drive, you could only do one thing at a time, and they certainly didn't have any internet.  They were also big and clunky, so they lived in one particular room and were shared with your family.  You turned them on to do a particular task, they were loud, and you turned them off when you were done.  Software came on floppy disks, which were fragile!  Never leave them outside their sleeves.  And never, ever touch their insides, or you could lose everything on the disk.

Computer games were also small.  You had Space Invaders, Pac-Man, Donkey Kong, Pitfall, Lode Runner, Q*bert.  Small, self-contained games that required "hand-eye coordination," but virtually no other skills.

But some games stood above the rest.  They had plot, they had character, they were grand in their vision of a world.

One of these was Below the Root.  Created by 3 people over the course of 4 months, it was published at the end of 1984 by Windham Classics as a children's adventure game.

This game had a real impact on me as a child.  Like interactive fiction, it had puzzles and plot and required reading.  I learned the word "avarice" from it (helpful to know to avoid being kidnapped), even though it had very few words: each character could only say one or two short sentences.  It was also a platformer, giving the world a visual flavor that kept my 10-year-old self engaged.  It rewarded patience, curiosity, and a bit of cleverness.  And it's fun to glide from screen to screen to explore the world!

40 years later, I still remember that game.  I played it obsessively in the 1980s as a kid, then again in the 2010s as an adult during a difficult time.  I didn't know about the books as a kid.  When I discovered that the game was based on Zilpha Keatley Snyder's Green-Sky trilogy, I read them: [*Below the Root*](https://openroadmedia.com/ebook/below-the-root/9781453271926), [*And All Between*](https://openroadmedia.com/ebook/and-all-between/9781453271933), and [*Until the Celebration*](https://openroadmedia.com/ebook/until-the-celebration/9781453271940).  I was amazed at how well the game captured the experience of being in that world: the shubas for gliding, the nids for sleeping, the engineered nonviolent "utopia" that the plot is based around.

I'd like to encourage you to take some time to play the game itself.  I've recreated a little computer for you to take a trip back to that basement rec room with the Commodore 64.  Take your time and try it out before reading the rest of this.

## This Port

This is the second time I've been motivated to restore this game for posterity.  In 2013, I spent 4 months creating a bootable ISO with a Linux unikernel, a C64 emulator, and a cracked version of the game.  It worked, but it was janky: the sound stuttered, and it required a VM.  It wasn't very accessible, even if it was easier than setting up an emulator yourself and swapping disks as you ventured into different areas.  I made a minimal release but basically shelved it.

Now you can play the game in any web browser, even on your phone or tablet!  I made sure the controls feel good on a phone and the game is winnable with all 5 characters.  I've played it through multiple times, with all 5 characters and across several platforms.  I fixed the bushes to be more like the original game, and less forgiving (sorry!  It broke 4/5 of my test wins, so I feel it too).  The map has been replicated cleanly from photos online, and it fills with real game screens as you explore.

There are other quality-of-life improvements, but I'll leave those for you to discover.  I've put at least a hundred hours of my own time, attention, and care into this restoration.  It's a version I can vouch for, of a work of art I love.

Preservation is one of my passions, and I take it seriously.  Please enjoy this game from my childhood, and check out my other work.
