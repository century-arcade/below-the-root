# Creatures

Everything the port needs to spawn, animate and talk to the 121 creatures
of Green-Sky.  Data: `docs/spec/data/creatures.json` (one entry per room
that has one) and `docs/spec/data/messages.json` (all 187 numbered strings
plus the fixed dialog strings).  Both are generated by
`tools/spec_creatures.py`.

Related: `docs/spec/world.md` (rooms, doors, tiles -- a room's
`creature_block` is the raw 18 bytes this file interprets),
`docs/spec/player.md` (the verb machinery, the object table, fall damage),
`docs/spec/time.md` (day, time-of-day slot, the economy),
`docs/spec/assets.md` (the `extras` sprite sheet).

Provenance tags like `[src: $9971]` name the 6502 routine a rule came
from.  A port never needs them.

## Vocabulary

- **creature** -- the single moving non-player figure a room may contain.
  A room has at most one, and it is described entirely by 18 bytes in the
  room's data block.
- **state id** -- 1..127, unique across the whole world.  It indexes the
  two per-creature persistent arrays.  121 ids are in use; 5, 11, 19, 23,
  25 and 75 are unused, and id 0 means "no creature".
- **species** -- 0..10, which sprite set and which of the two hard-coded
  behaviour splits apply.
- **kind** -- the creature's social role: gift-giver, merchant, blesser,
  ambusher and so on.  Named in the JSON; the raw byte is kept as
  `kind_raw` for cross-checking only.
- **cell** -- a playfield character cell.  The playfield is 40 columns x
  20 rows; creatures and the player are positioned in cells and drawn as
  a 24x42 pixel figure standing on the bottom of that cell.
- **tick** -- one video frame.  The creature AI runs once per tick while
  the game loop is live (60/s, see `docs/spec/README.md`).  It does
  **not** run at the player's step rate, so creature speed is independent
  of everything the player does. `[src: $A01E]`

## Species

| id | name | role | figure | rooms |
|----|------|------|--------|-------|
| 0 | kindar_adult_long_hair | person | long-haired adult | 19 |
| 1 | kindar_adult_short_hair | person | short-haired adult | 19 |
| 2 | erdling_adult_long_hair | person | long-haired adult | 12 |
| 3 | erdling_adult_short_hair | person | short-haired adult | 17 |
| 4 | child_long_hair | person | child | 7 |
| 5 | child_short_hair | person | child | 7 |
| 6 | lapan | animal | rabbit-like | 5 |
| 7 | sima | animal | curl-tailed climber | 5 |
| 8 | snake | hostile animal | snake | 9 |
| 9 | spider | hostile animal | many-legged crawler | 15 |
| 10 | ol_zhaan | person | robed figure with a staff | 6 |

The names for 6/7 come from the manual's dictionary matched to the
sprites -- a lapan "looks like a rabbit", a sima is "small tree-dwelling"
-- and 8/9 from the walkthrough's "Beware of SPIDERS and SNAKES as these
will make your character fall", which is exactly what those two species
do.  None of the four names is stated by the code; the other seven are
descriptive labels, not names the game uses.  Species 0/1 and 2/3 are the
same two adult figures in different colours; the race split is carried by
the response gate, not by the sprite.

The code branches on species number in exactly two places:

- species 8 and 9 use the contact rule instead of the stop-for-the-player
  rule, and never take the long idle pause; `[src: $99C7, $9A71]`
- the wand of Befal costs 5 spirit limit against every species except
  6-9, where it costs 1. `[src: $938A]`

Species 10 is the robed Ol-zhaan, in six rooms: the Hermit (41), D'ol
Falla (71), D'ol Neshom (191), Vatar (492) and two who are only there to
talk (36 and 63).

### Sprites

Each species owns six 24x21 sprite records in the `extras` sheet:
records `2 + 6*species` through `7 + 6*species`.  They pair up into three
frames, `[top, bottom]`, stacked to make one 24x42 figure. `[src: $9887]`

Only the left-facing frames are stored.  The right-facing set is the
horizontal mirror, produced once when the creature is loaded; a port
should just flip the sprite.

The whole figure is drawn in **one colour**, the descriptor's
`sprite_color` (a C64 palette index 0-15).  There is no second colour and
no outline.

Placement, in the same coordinate convention the player sprite uses:

```
bottom_half.y = row_to_y[row] - 10
top_half.y    = bottom_half.y - 21
both.x        = col_to_x[col]
```

with `row_to_y[r] = 8*r + 48` and `col_to_x[c] = 8*c + 16` in the
hardware's sprite coordinates, where the top-left playfield cell begins at
(24, 50).  Reduced to cells: the figure stands with its feet exactly on
the bottom edge of cell (col, row), horizontally centred on that column,
three cells wide and five and a quarter cells tall -- so it overhangs one
cell either side and four cells upward.  The player sprite uses the same
two tables and the same offsets. `[src: $9B37, tables built at $8245/$8252]`

## The descriptor

The 18 bytes a room block carries become the creature.  Field names below
are the JSON's; see `docs/spec/world.md` for where they sit in the block.

| field | meaning |
|-------|---------|
| `species`, `sprite_color` | sprite set and colour |
| `gate` | `{stat, level}`: which standing to test and the level required |
| `start.col_random_span`, `gait` | random start offset, and slow/fast walk |
| `start.col`, `start.row` | spawn cell before the random offset |
| `patrol.turn_col_low/high` | the two columns the creature turns at |
| `dialog.gate_passed.*` | the four message numbers used when the gate passes |
| `dialog.gate_failed.*` | the same four for when it fails |
| `params.offers_item_class` / `offers: "nid"` | what SPEAK grants permission for |
| `state_id` | index into the persistent arrays |
| `kind` | the social role |

The species and colour share one byte, and that byte being **zero** is
how a room says it has no creature; every rule below checks it first.
(Species 0 with a non-zero colour is a perfectly ordinary creature.)
`creatures.json` lists only the 121 rooms that have one.

## Spawn

Runs on every room entry, and again whenever the game re-enters the room
loop after a door. `[src: $980A]`

```
spawn_creature():
    active = false
    hide creature sprites
    col = row = 50                       # off-screen parking value
    if banished[state_id]: return        # zapped with the wand, gone for good
    if room has no creature: return

    if kind is an ambusher:
        if (contact[state_id] & 0x1F) != current_time_slot:
            if random(4) != 0: return                 # 3 in 4: does not appear
            contact[state_id] = (contact[state_id] & 0xE0) | current_time_slot

    load the species sprite set, tinted sprite_color
    col = start.col + random(start.col_random_span)   # 0 .. span-1
    row = start.row
    facing = random pick of left or right
    frame = frame 0 of that facing
    frame_counter = 0
    frames_to_next = 4
    step_phase = 0; frame_offset = 0; just_turned = false
    active = true
```

Notes:

- The random column offset is uniform over `0 .. span-1`; `span` is never
  0 in the shipped data (the original does a rejection loop on a 5-bit
  random, which would hang if it were). `[src: $98FD]`
- In every shipped room `start.col` >= `patrol.turn_col_low` and
  `start.col + span - 1` <= `patrol.turn_col_high`, so a creature always
  spawns inside its own patrol range.
- **Ambushers and the time slot.** The remembered slot starts at 0, and
  time slot 0 is EARLY MORNING, so an ambusher that has never been seen
  appears with certainty in early morning and only one time in four in
  any other slot.  Once it has appeared it will appear again in that same
  slot every time until a 1-in-4 roll moves the slot on.
- The original re-reads the room block from disk at this point, because
  the sprite copy overwrites the memory the block lives under.  That is
  why walking into a room with a creature is visibly slower on real
  hardware; a port has no reason to reproduce it. `[src: $98DE]`

## Despawn

- Leaving a room by any route hides the sprites; the next room's entry
  runs `spawn_creature` again, so nothing persists between rooms except
  the two saved arrays. `[src: $9662]`
- The wand of Befal (below) removes a creature permanently.
- A creature is *never* removed by talking to it, paying it, or by time.

## Movement

One pass per tick, and it does nothing at all until the frame counter
reaches the creature's current period. `[src: $9971]`

```
creature_step():
    if not active: return
    frame_counter += 1
    if frame_counter != frames_to_next: return       # equality, not >=
    frame_counter = 0

    tile_ahead = tile at (col + facing, row)
    tile_below = tile at (col, row + 1)

    if step_phase == 1:                              # mid-stride: finish it
        half_step(); return

    if tile_below has no support:                    # gravity
        row += 1
        frames_to_next = 4
        reposition(); return

    if species is snake or spider:
        contact_check()                              # may knock the player down
    else if kind is an ambusher:
        ambush_check()                               # may end the scene
    else:
        # stop and wait to be spoken to
        if player is 1 or 2 cells ahead in the facing direction
           and |player.row - row| <= 1: return

    turn_or_step()

turn_or_step():
    if not just_turned and (col == patrol.turn_col_low
                            or col == patrol.turn_col_high):
        facing = -facing
        frame = frame 0 of the new facing
        frames_to_next = turn_pause[gait]            # slow 12, fast 8
        just_turned = true
        reposition(); return

    if random(8) == 0:                               # 1 in 8: skip this step
        if species is snake or spider: return        # they just lose the step
        frames_to_next = random(128)                 # 0 reads as a 256-frame pause
        return

    if just_turned:                                  # the tick right after a turn
        half_step(); return
    if random(16) == 0:                              # 1 in 16: turn anyway
        turn (same as above)
        return
    half_step()

half_step():
    just_turned = false
    step_phase ^= 1
    frames_to_next = half_step_frames[gait][step_phase]
    if step_phase == 1:                              # the "up" half of the stride
        frame = walk frame 1 + frame_offset          # frame 1, then frame 2, ...
        frame_offset = 2 - frame_offset
    else:                                            # the "down" half: the cell changes
        frame = walk frame 0
        col += facing
        if tile_ahead was solid: row -= 1            # creatures climb single steps
    reposition()
```

Periods, in ticks:

| gait | after a turn | entering phase 0 | entering phase 1 |
|------|--------------|------------------|------------------|
| slow | 12 | 8 | 12 |
| fast | 8 | 6 | 10 |

So a slow creature advances one column every 20 ticks (~0.4 s) and a fast
one every 16.  The animation cycle is frame 1, 0, 2, 0, 1, 0, 2, ... with
the column advancing on every frame-0 half.

Gravity is the *only* piece of player physics creatures obey.  They do
not swim, glide, climb ropes, fall damage, or interact with objects.
They can walk off the edge of the room: nothing clamps the column, and a
creature whose patrol columns let it reach column 0 or 39 and keep going
will read an empty tile and start falling (see GAP below).

## Contact -- snakes and spiders

Species 8 and 9 only.  `[src: $9A06]`

```
contact_check():
    if the player is already knocked down: return
    if player.row not in {row, row - 1}: return
    if player.col not in {col, col + facing, col + 2*facing}: return
    request a 10-row knock-down on the player
```

The knock-down request is the same one a long fall produces: on the
player's next step it trips the fall-damage test, which is what the
walkthrough means by "Beware of SPIDERS and SNAKES as these will make
your character fall."  It can also tear a carried shuba.  See
`docs/spec/player.md`.

Snakes and spiders are entirely silent: all eight of their message slots
are 0, so SPEAK and PENSE both answer `NO RESPONSE`.

## Ambush

The twelve ambushers are ordinary-looking people -- species 0/1 for the
followers of D'ol Salaat, species 2/3 for the Nekom -- who appear only
sometimes and end the scene on contact. `[src: $9AFA, $B320]`

```
ambush_check():
    if player.col not in {col - 2, col - 1, col, col + 1}: return
    if |player.row - row| > 1: return
    trigger the creature's event and drop out of the room loop
```

| kind | event |
|------|-------|
| `ambusher_kidnap_salaat` | print `kidnapped_salaat`, wake in room 28 at column 21, row 15 |
| `ambusher_attack_salaat` | print `attacked_salaat`, sent home to your own nid-place |
| `ambusher_kidnap_nekom` | print `kidnapped_nekom`, wake in room 59 at column 19, row 15 |
| `ambusher_attack_nekom` | print `attacked_nekom`, sent home to your own nid-place |

Both "attacked" strings say TIME HAS PASSED; the time cost itself is in
`docs/spec/time.md`.  The two kidnap destinations are the same rooms the
REST traps use.

Ambushers have no speech and no pense message -- only an emotion, and the
emotion is the tell: 31 DEVIOUSNESS, 33 INSANE FANATICISM, 30 HOSTILITY,
32 RAGE.  That is exactly the manual's advice: "When encountering a
Green-Sky inhabitant for the first time, pense from a distance.  If you
read a negative emotion, such as rage or anger, try to avoid crossing
that character's path."

## Adjacency and the response gate

Two predicates the dialog verbs share.

```
adjacent():                                     # [src: $4364]
    return  creature.col in {player.col + player.facing,
                             player.col + 2*player.facing}
        and |creature.row - player.row| <= 1
        and creature.facing != player.facing     # looking at each other

gate_passes():                                  # [src: $43C2]
    return player.standing[creature.gate.stat] >= creature.gate.level
```

`standing` is the pair of values set once at character creation and never
changed: standing with the Kindar and standing with the Erdlings (see
`docs/spec/player.md`).  The gate picks one of the two and a threshold.

The gate levels that occur: 0 (109 creatures), 1, 2, 3 and 4.  Level 0
passes for every playable character, so only 12 creatures can ever give
their "gate failed" lines -- eleven gift-givers and one plain talker --
and which of the 12 actually fail depends on who you are playing.  Those
same 12 are exactly the creatures whose gate-failed slots are populated;
no level-0 creature carries a single one, so nothing in the fail set is
dead data.

Note the asymmetry: SPEAK, BUY, SELL and OFFER require adjacency; PENSE
EMOTIONS does not (emotions work anywhere in the room), while PENSE
MESSAGES does.

## Dialog

Every message the creatures say is a 1-based number into a single table
of 187 strings.  Nothing is random and nothing is generated: the room
block names exactly eight numbers and the gate chooses which four are
live.  A creature with a 0 in a slot answers `NO RESPONSE` for that slot.

Four sinks, all in the five-row text area under the playfield:

| slot | verb | row, column |
|------|------|-------------|
| `speak_line1` | SPEAK | 21, 1 |
| `speak_line2` | SPEAK | 22, 1 |
| `emotion` | PENSE EMOTIONS | 21, 10 (after the `EMOTION:` label) |
| `message` | PENSE MESSAGES | 24, 1 (label `MESSAGE:` on row 23) |

Text is uppercase ASCII written straight into cells with no wrapping
logic: a line runs off the right edge into column 0 of the next row.
Logical lines are therefore 40 characters wide starting in column 1, and
every multi-line string in the data is padded so the 40th character is a
space.  No string in the numbered table is longer than 39 characters, so
only the fixed strings ever wrap.

### SPEAK

```
verb_speak():                                   # [src: $3C48]
    clear the text area
    if no creature or not adjacent:
        print "SPEAK WITH WHOM?"; return

    if gate_passes() and kind == gift_giver:
        # (a) is there anything left in this room to give?
        if offers is an item class:
            if no object of any class is lying on the ground in this room:
                print "I HAVE NOTHING MORE TO GIVE"; return
        # (b) one gift per day per creature
        if (contact[state_id] & 0x7F) == today:
            print "COME BACK TOMORROW, MY FRIEND"; return

    lines = gate_passes() ? dialog.gate_passed.speak : dialog.gate_failed.speak
    if lines[0] == 0:
        print "NO RESPONSE"; return
    print message lines[0] on row 21
    if lines[1] != 0: print message lines[1] on row 22

    contact[state_id] = (contact[state_id] & 0x80) | today   # every creature

    if gate_passes():
        if kind in {gift_giver, blesser, key_revealer, any rest_trap}:
            take_permission = true
        if kind == key_revealer:
            falla_key_revealed = true

    if kind == blesser and bit 7 of contact[state_id] is clear:
        set bit 7 of contact[state_id]
        spirit_limit += 5
        spirit_energy = spirit_limit
        play a random tune
        run the spirit-power / vision sequence
```

Reading order matters:

- the gift checks (a) and (b) run **only** for `gift_giver`, and only
  when the gate passes.  Every other kind talks unconditionally.
- the "is there anything left" test does **not** look for an object of
  the offered class -- any object still on the ground in the room
  satisfies it.  Once a room has been stripped bare, its gift-giver says
  `I HAVE NOTHING MORE TO GIVE` whatever it was offering. `[src: $3D4E]`
- a creature whose `offers` is `"nid"` skips that test entirely.
- the day stamp is written for *every* creature that says anything, but
  only `gift_giver` ever reads it back.  So plain talkers, blessers and
  merchants can be spoken to as often as you like.
- the blesser's +5 is granted **outside** the gate test -- a blesser
  whose gate failed would still bless you.  It never comes up: all five
  blessers have a level-0 gate, and all five have empty gate-failed
  slots, so a failing blesser could not get as far as the reward anyway
  (an empty first speech line ends the verb at `NO RESPONSE`).
- the walkthrough says these characters give 5 spirit "pensing or
  talking".  Only SPEAK does it; pensing a blesser costs energy and gives
  nothing back.

### PENSE

```
verb_pense():                                   # [src: $407B]
    clear the text area
    if no creature: print "PENSE WHOM?"; return
    if spirit_limit < 5: print "YOU LACK THE SPRIT SKILL"; return   # sic
    if spirit_energy == 0: print "YOU NEED MORE SPIRIT ENERGY"; return

    print "EMOTION:" on row 21
    n = gate_passes() ? dialog.gate_passed.emotion : dialog.gate_failed.emotion
    if n == 0: print "NO RESPONSE"; return       # costs nothing
    print message n at row 21 column 10
    spirit_energy -= 1

    # the MESSAGE half is a separate, stricter skill
    if spirit_limit < 10: return
    if spirit_energy == 0: return
    if not adjacent(): return
    if kind == pensable_animal and bit 7 of contact[state_id] is set: return

    print "MESSAGE:" on row 23
    n = gate_passes() ? dialog.gate_passed.message : dialog.gate_failed.message
    if n == 0: print "NO RESPONSE" on row 23; return    # emotion still cost 1
    print message n on row 24
    spirit_energy -= 1

    if kind == pensable_animal:
        set bit 7 of contact[state_id]
        spirit_limit += 1
        spirit_energy = spirit_limit
        play a random tune
        animals_pensed += 1
        if animals_pensed == 5: run the spirit-power / vision sequence
```

A full pense costs 2 spirit energy, one for each half; an empty slot
costs nothing for that half.

The ten pensable animals (species 6 and 7) all carry the same pair:
emotion 152 `WARMTH`, message 151 `THE SPIRIT RISES WITHIN`.  Each gives
+1 spirit limit once per quest, and the congratulation and vision fire on
the fifth animal pensed, whichever one that turns out to be.

### The spirit ladder from creatures

Five blessers at +5 (rooms 19, 41, 116, 191, 492) and ten animals at +1
is +35 over a quest, on top of a starting limit of 0-10.  The skill
ladder tops out at 30 (KINIPORT YOUR BODY), so a player who finds
everything has room to spare -- and a wand of Befal spent on a person
costs 5 of it back.

### BUY and SELL

Both refuse outright unless the adjacent creature is a `merchant`:
`THERE IS NO MERCHANT HERE`, then `NO RESPONSE` if the merchant is not
adjacent. `[src: $4338]`

The economy numbers -- one token in each direction, which classes are
sellable, the carry-weight test -- belong to `docs/spec/time.md`.  What
matters here is the creature hook: BUY does **not** hand over an object.
It spends a token and sets `take_permission`, and the merchant's
`stock_item_class` is what TAKE will then accept.  The stock is the same
`offers_item_class` field the gift-givers use.

The eight merchants and their stock:

| room | stock |
|------|-------|
| 26 | a roast lapan |
| 37 | fruit & nuts |
| 38 | a trencher beak |
| 39 | a honeylamp |
| 46 | wissenberries |
| 64 | a shuba |
| 68 | a vine rope |
| 69 | pan bread |

Each says its pitch as line 1 and `ONLY A TOKEN` (message 127) as line 2.

### OFFER

OFFER cycles the carried inventory and then dispatches on the creature's
**state id**, not on its kind -- there are exactly three creatures in the
world that accept anything. `[src: $43CC]`

| state id | room | accepts | result |
|----------|------|---------|--------|
| 73 | 464 | a shuba or a vine rope | the quest ends; see `docs/spec/time.md` |
| 52 | 352 | wissenberries | one passage now; the second one ever offered opens the door permanently |
| 53 | 32 | a token | one passage now, every time |

Anything else offered to one of those three: `THAT WON'T HELP`.  Anything
offered to any other creature: `NO RESPONSE`.

Both guard paths consume the offered object and set the per-visit
`door_permission` flag.

## The take/rest hooks

Creatures never hand anything over.  They only set `take_permission`, and
the player's own verbs spend it. `[src: $AD52, $AC15]`

- **TAKE** of an object lying in the room: outdoors it always works, and
  indoors it always works in your own nid-place and in four rooms named
  by number in the code: 28, 59, 75 and 81.  Anywhere
  else indoors -- including every room numbered 256 or above, which skips
  that whitelist -- it needs `take_permission` set *and* the object's
  class to equal the creature's `offers_item_class`, else
  `IT WAS NOT OFFERED TO YOU`.  A successful take clears the permission.
- **REST** away from your own nid-place needs `take_permission` set *and*
  the creature's `offers` to be `"nid"`, else `NO ONE OFFERED YOU A NID`.
  That is on top of REST's own requirement of standing indoors under a
  nid tile.  A successful rest does *not* clear the permission.
- **D'ol Falla's key** is the exception: its class is exempt from the
  offer test entirely once `falla_key_revealed` is set, which only the
  one `key_revealer` in room 71 does.

So `offers` is the whole gift economy: 22 gift-givers, the five blessers,
the eight merchants' stock, D'ol Falla, and the twelve creatures that
offer a nid.

Four of the five blessers carry class 0, the spirit bell, and only D'ol
Neshom's room (191) actually contains one -- which is why his line is
`I GRANT YOU THE SPIRIT BELL`.  The fifth, Vatar, offers fruit & nuts.
D'ol Falla also carries class 0, but her key is exempt from the class
test, so the field never matters for her.

## Nid events -- the REST traps

Four kinds offer a nid and spring something when you sleep in it.  The
event runs after the rest has restored the player, and only if the
creature has not been banished. `[src: $ACAE]`

| kind | room(s) | effect |
|------|---------|--------|
| `rest_trap_steal_tokens` | 20, 62 | every carried token vanishes |
| `rest_trap_steal_shubas` | 49 | every carried shuba vanishes |
| `rest_trap_kidnap_salaat` | 29 | print `kidnapped_salaat`, wake in room 28 at (21,15) |
| `rest_trap_kidnap_nekom` | 40, 42 | print `kidnapped_nekom`, wake in room 59 at (19,15) |

Their emotions give them away in advance: AVARICE, GREED, DECEIT, GUILE,
FURTIVENESS.  Their pense messages are blunter still -- `SLEEP WELL,
FOOL`, `THE NEKOM WILL PAY ME WELL`.

## Door creatures

Two creatures stand in front of a door and refuse to let you through.
When the player walks into a door tile, before the destination is read:
`[src: $96B9]`

```
door_lock_check():
    if kind == gate_guard_pass and gate_a_open: pass
    else if kind == gate_guard_token and gate_b_open: pass
    else if door_permission: pass
    else: print "THE DOOR IS LOCKED"; cancel the door
```

- `gate_a_open` and `gate_b_open` are the *banished* bits of the two
  guards themselves (state ids 52 and 53), so there are two ways to open
  each door for good: hit the guard with the wand of Befal, or -- for the
  wissenberry guard only -- offer her a second wissenberry.
- `door_permission` is set by any accepted OFFER and cleared on every
  room load, so it buys exactly one passage.
- The check reads the *room's* creature descriptor, not a live creature,
  so it still applies when the guard has been banished -- but banishing
  her sets the flag that opens the door, so the effect is the same.

The two doors are the entrance to the underground, in series: the
wissenberry guard stands in room 352 on the surface and her door leads to
room 0; from room 0 you walk south into room 32, where the token guard's
door leads to room 384, the first room below the root.  That is exactly
the walkthrough's route -- "To get permission to enter the doorway to the
underground, OFFER the guard Wissenberries.  OFFER the guard inside a
token, you will then be allowed to enter the underground".  Room numbers
cross-checked against `docs/spec/data/rooms.json`; only the outer door can
be unlocked permanently by dialogue (see the GAP below).

## The wand of Befal

USE of a wand of Befal on an adjacent creature removes it from the game.
`[src: $933A]`

```
banish():
    if state_id == 0 or banished[state_id]: fail, the wand does nothing
    if creature.col not in {player.col, player.col + facing,
                            player.col + 2*facing}: fail
    if |creature.row - player.row| > 1: fail
    banished[state_id] = true
    play the zap sound; hide the creature; active = false
    spirit_limit -= (species is 6..9) ? 1 : 5      # floored at 0
    spirit_energy = 0
```

Note the adjacency rule here is *not* the dialog one: the creature may be
in the player's own column, and it does not have to be facing back.

The consequences are permanent and saved: the creature never spawns
again, its REST trap never fires again, and if it was one of the two
gate guards its door is open for the rest of the quest.  The cost is
steep against a person -- 5 of the spirit limit you spent the whole game
raising, plus all your current energy.

## Persistent state

One 256-byte page, split into two 128-entry arrays indexed by state id.
Both are cleared when a quest starts and both sit inside the block a
saved game writes.  The split is why state ids stop at 127: id 128 would
put the first array's entry on top of the second array's entry for id 0.

**`banished[id]`** -- one bit.  Set only by the wand of Befal.  Read by
spawn, by the REST trap dispatcher, and (for ids 52 and 53) by the door
lock check.

**`contact[id]`** -- three overlapping meanings, which never collide
because no creature has both dialogue and an ambush:

| bits | meaning |
|------|---------|
| 0-6 | the day this creature was last spoken to; 0 = never.  Days run 1..51, so it always fits |
| 0-4 | for ambushers only: the time-of-day slot (0-7) in which it last appeared |
| 7 | the once-per-quest spirit gift has been collected -- by SPEAK from a blesser, by PENSE MESSAGES from a pensable animal |

Three more flags sit outside the per-creature arrays.  All three are part
of the saved game (they live in the zero page the save block copies), but
two of them are wiped so often that saving them barely matters:

- `take_permission` -- something has been offered.  Cleared by a
  successful TAKE **and by every room load**, so an offer has to be
  spoken for and taken in the same visit: leave the room and the offer is
  gone (though you can just ask again). `[src: $8BB7, $ADF2]`
- `door_permission` -- a guard has been paid.  Also cleared on every room
  load, so it buys exactly one passage. `[src: $8BB9]`
- `falla_key_revealed` -- D'ol Falla has been spoken to.  Cleared only
  when a new quest starts, so it is effectively quest-lifetime.

## GAPs

- **RESOLVED: `$0A88`.** One byte inside the creature's runtime variable
  block is allocated and never read or written by anything in the 64K
  image.  A hole in the layout; see `world.md` §10.
- **GAP: no bounds on creature position.** Nothing clamps a creature's
  column to 0..39 or its row to 0..19.  A creature only ever turns at its
  two patrol columns, and in all 121 shipped rooms the patrol range is
  inside the playfield, so it never comes up -- but a port that lets a
  creature leave the range (through the step-up rule, which changes the
  row without any range test) will read tiles outside the room.  The
  original returns a blank tile for column -1, column 40 and negative
  rows, which has no support, so such a creature falls forever.
- **GAP: creatures share the player's crawl flag.** The tile-property
  routine is shared, and one tile code (the "object resting in a nid"
  code) counts as solid ground *only while the player is crawling*.  A
  creature standing over that tile therefore gains and loses its footing
  according to what the player is doing.  No shipped room appears to
  place a creature there; treating the tile as never-supporting for
  creatures is the safer port. `[src: $9DD8]`
- **GAP: `$09F1` = `$30`, `$90`, `$F0` (31 creatures).** *Resolved*: they
  reach no special handler at all.  They talk exactly like any other
  creature -- all 31 have a speech line and an emotion, and 27 also have
  a pense message -- but being non-zero excludes them from both gift
  branches, so they never trigger the once-a-day limit and can never
  grant a TAKE.
  `plain_talker` is the right name; "hits no handler" and "plain talker"
  were describing the same thing.  Whether the three distinct byte values
  once meant three different things is not answerable from the binary:
  no code reads the high nibble of any of them.
- **GAP: `gate_b_open` (`$2335`) has no dialogue writer.** *Resolved*: it
  is read by the door lock check and no OFFER path ever sets it.  The
  door is **not** permanently locked, and `door_permission` is not the
  only key: the wand of Befal writes the same array, so banishing the
  room-32 guard opens her door for good.  Otherwise the token is a toll
  paid on every single visit, in contrast to the room-352 guard whose
  second wissenberry opens her door permanently.  The room-32 guard's own
  line -- `WAIT A MINUTE.  I WANT TOKENS.`, plural -- reads as if the
  asymmetry is deliberate, and so does the topology: the permanently
  unlockable door is the outer one on a route you walk repeatedly, while
  the toll door is the last step into the underground.  But the manual
  does not say so, and a one-line omission is just as likely.
- **GAP: `offers_item_class` on creatures that can never use it.** 84 of
  the 121 descriptors carry a 0 in that byte, which is a *valid* class
  (the spirit bell).  For most of them the kind never sets
  `take_permission`, so the byte is inert; four of the five blessers are
  the interesting case, since they *do* set it and so would permit taking
  a spirit bell -- but only D'ol Neshom's room contains one.  A port
  should read the field only for the kinds listed under "The take/rest
  hooks".
- **GAP: the numbered table has three dead entries.** 34 (`NOTHING`) and
  two `*` placeholders at 135 and 171 are referenced by nothing.  They
  have to stay in place to keep the numbering.
